# PDP playbook: zone-by-zone patterns

The pattern library distilled from ~25 before/after teardowns by a specialist e-commerce CRO agency, plus their written frameworks. Each pattern lists **why** it works, so you can judge when it applies. Evidence strength:
- **Firm**: seen in most cases and/or stated as a rule with a reason.
- **Default**: strong tendency; deviate when the page context argues otherwise.
- **Judgement**: depends on context; reason it out each time.

## 1. The base sequence (mobile PDP) — Firm

For information-led (functional) purchases:

1. One short announcement bar
2. Header: hamburger left, logo centre, search + cart right (no account icon on mobile)
3. Badges / identity label above the title (1–3)
4. Product title (clear: what it is + key qualifier)
5. 1–2 line description (benefit / why it works / who it's for)
6. Rating with underlined, clickable review count
7. Price with value context (quantity, duration, per-unit)
8. Gallery: hero answers the top doubt; arrow + sneak peek; thumbnails when educational
9. 3 benefit bullets (when variants aren't visual; otherwise after the CTA)
10. Options: "Select X: [current value]"; visual tiles; pack/size upsell; subscription
11. Quantity stepper inline with the add-to-cart (or dropped when packs do the job)
12. Service USPs directly under the CTA (shipping time, free shipping threshold, returns)
13. A short review / expert quote / social proof
14. "Why you'll love it" bullets or brand/positioning block (when not shown above)
15. Question-style accordions
16. Cross-sell ("Complete your routine / look", "Works well with")
17. UGC / "See it in action" video row

**Why the title block goes above the gallery:** the gallery must sit next to the options so that when a shopper changes a colour or flavour, the image updates on the same screen. Price, reviews and description placed between gallery and options force scrolling back up. Moving context above the image also puts the price where shoppers look for it (next to the name).

**Exception — aesthetic purchases** (home décor, fashion, jewellery design): the image persuades most, so the gallery may come first and larger, followed closely by a compact title/price block. Still keep the image ≤ 60–70% of the viewport and the title/price within the first fold.

## 2. Announcement bar and header

- **One bar, short height. — Firm.** Merge stacked bars; rotate 2–3 messages behind arrows if needed.
- **Content must support the purchase on this page. — Firm.** Best messages: free-shipping threshold (raises AOV, removes cost anxiety), free samples/gifts on every order, free returns / money-back guarantee (removes last-minute hesitation), social proof ("Trusted by 2.4M customers"), a sale that applies to the products shown. Price tier decides which leads: consumables → threshold/samples; high-ticket → returns/guarantee.
- **Avoid:** unrelated campaigns (a holiday gift shop, a collab launch), codes shoppers must remember (prefer bundle/threshold messages; if a code stays, show the post-code price next to the price), links that pull shoppers away ("Shop now") unless they resolve fit (a product-finder quiz).
- **Threshold placement:** set just above a single-item order so one item doesn't qualify (₹1,299 vs an ₹837 product; $50 vs a $30 set). Too high and it adds cost anxiety.
- **Styling:** underline links; never use the CTA colour. Scrolling tickers are acceptable; a static bar with arrows is a mild preference.
- **Urgency must be true.** "Sale ends tonight" only if it does; sale messaging should state the offer, the reason and the end date.
- **Header:** hamburger left of the logo; search + cart icons right; remove the account icon on mobile; a giant "MY CART" button becomes an icon (especially if it shares the CTA colour).

## 3. Title block (above the gallery)

- **Badges above the title. — Firm.** 1–3 pills, 2–3 words. The slot holds the single most decision-relevant qualifier for this product, which varies:
  - concern it solves ("Dry skin", "Acne-prone", "Gut detox")
  - top objections pre-empted ("No white cast", "Non-sticky", "0g caffeine")
  - core differentiators ("Nothing artificial", "Non-toxic, plastic free")
  - pack/format ("Pack of 8", "Kona Blend", "Medium roast · Ground")
  Badges take load off the description.
- **Title says exactly what it is. — Firm.** Include what a shopper can't assume: form/texture (syrup, powder, stick sachet, gel), flavour or scent, pack count, purpose ("Office Chair"), audience where relevant ("for Women"). Don't rely on a brand name alone ("The Ariel 1.0" → "Ariel 1.0 – The Original Office Chair"). Avoid keyword-stuffed titles; keep SEO keywords in the meta title.
- **1–2 line description. — Firm.** Its job varies by category: why it works (mechanism + benefit), who it's for and what changes, or a sensory "imagine using it" line for food/beverage. Never 3+ lines in the first fold.
- **Rating with underlined count. — Firm** (when there are ~100+ reviews; otherwise consider omitting from the first fold). Clicking scrolls to reviews.
- **Price with value context. — Firm.** Quantity (ml, oz, g, units, "Pack of 6"), duration ("30-day supply"), or per-use ("lasts 60 applications", "24 doses", "$2.28/day"). For high-ticket: a spec line under the title ("1.8 ct · VS1-2 · GH"). For multi-item sets, say what's inside ("2 × 250 ml").
- **Headline price must name its purchase option.** If a subscription price is shown at the top, label it ("$28 with subscription"). Prefer showing the one-time price at the top and the subscription price in the options.
- **Discount visibility:** a faint "40% OFF" does no work; use a clear "Save 40%" pill.

## 4. Gallery

- **The hero image answers the category's #1 doubt. — Judgement, reasoned each time.**
  - Efficacy doubt (does it work?) → proof on the image: clinical stat bar, press badge (dark-spot serum).
  - Visual doubt (white cast, finish, shade on skin) → a lifestyle/application shot that shows the proof (sunscreen on a face).
  - "What exactly am I getting?" (product hidden in a box) → show the texture or contents (stick sachets beside the box).
  - Desire / sensory (coffee, snacks) → an attention-catching lifestyle or appetite shot.
  - Iconic packaging → a packshot can carry the attention.
  - Furniture/décor → room context showing scale and use.
- **Badges on the hero:** best seller, most re-ordered, award, "As seen on" press. Especially valuable for paid/cold traffic.
- **A stat bar on the image** for a real, substantiated result ("96% saw no white cast").
- **Arrow + sneak peek. — Firm.** Show a sliver of the next image so swiping is obvious; the peek can tease proof (nutrition panel, on-skin swatch).
- **Thumbnails. — Default.** Use when the gallery educates (ingredients, how-to, before/after, nutrition label, comparisons, video); they signal there's more to learn. Dots are acceptable when space is tight. Replace bare "1/7" counters.
- **Gallery content:** benefits, key ingredients, how to use, before/afters, reviews, nutrition label (ingestibles), comparison ("us vs them"), scale, UGC, upsells. It's the most-viewed and most-clicked section; make it the one place to learn everything. In the prototype, the gallery is a numbered set of placeholders with an image brief; the auditor plans the shots, the brand produces them.
- **Gallery length:** trim to the images that do a job (~5–8); 30 dots is too many.
- **Image size:** ≤ 60–70% of the viewport. Reduce oversized heroes; never a full-bleed image that pushes title, price and CTA off the first two screens.
- **Zoom:** keep for physical goods where detail matters.

## 5. Copy: bullets, descriptions, paragraphs

- **Bullets over paragraphs. — Firm.** Three bullets, one line each on mobile, ordered longest to shortest. Pattern: two benefits, the third with proof; or benefit + mechanism pairs ("Niacinamide to brighten while it protects"). Three words per bullet for feature lists in two columns.
- **Bullet placement depends on variants. — Firm.**
  - No visual variants → bullets directly after the image.
  - Visual variants (colour, shade, flavour imagery) → the selector goes directly under the gallery; compact content between is fine only if the selector stays on the same screen as the image. Benefits move into the description above the image or a "Why you'll love it" block after the CTA.
- **Don't duplicate.** Bullets that repeat the description or badges waste first-fold space.
- **Paragraph limits:** ≤ 2 lines in the first fold; ≤ 3 lines anywhere; left-aligned, never centred long text.
- **Copy pillars:** clear, easy to read, distinctive. Speak to pain points and wants. Name the shopper's history with failed alternatives when relevant ("No more trial and error").
- **Brand voice:** condense, don't neutralise.
- **A visible, truncated description with "Read more"** is an acceptable alternative to an accordion for the main description.
- **Persuasive copy stays visible; reference copy can collapse.** Trust-building brand copy shouldn't hide in an accordion.

## 6. Options zone

Its two jobs: get visitors to engage with options (engagement lifts add-to-carts), and raise AOV/LTV via packs and subscriptions.

- **Action-verb labels with the current value. — Firm.** "Select Size: 30 oz", "Select Flavour: Frosted Strawberry". The verb prompts exploration; the echo confirms the choice.
- **Visual tiles instead of dropdowns. — Firm** when variants look or taste different (flavours, colours, ground vs whole bean). Dropdown only when > 4 options.
- **2–4 options. — Firm.** More than four overwhelms. If options are really combinations (shampoo type × conditioner pairing = 12 tiles), **decompose** into two selectors of 3–4 rather than hiding the range.
- **Remove dead selectors.** A selector with one option ("Size: 12oz") becomes text in the price line.
- **Every option has a reason to be chosen.** Purpose labels ("Best Results", "Travel Friendly", "Best Value", "For Best Results"), savings ("Save $30", "10% off"), per-unit price ("$3.60/can", "$1.94/day"). Check labels stay true across combinations (a "Best Value" 4-pack shouldn't be beaten by a 1-jar subscription).
- **Packs before the CTA; cross-sells after. — Firm.** Same-product upsells (bigger size, multi-pack, subscription, variety pack) go in the options. Other products go after the CTA. A bundle framed as a version ("Water Cream + Aqua Sun Gel") can sit in the options.
- **Variety pack for F&B with flavours. — Default.** Usually the best seller with new visitors; reduces the risk of picking wrong; increases repeat purchase. Place it next to the default flavour.
- **Subscriptions. — Firm for consumables.** Present as equal, readable cards (one-time and subscribe), with a "Save X%" badge and 3–4 benefits in a compact grid (save %, free shipping, skip/pause/cancel anytime, rewards). Give a reason beyond discount where possible ("ship around your cycle"). Delivery cadence shown as text when fixed; drop dropdowns at the commit point when possible. "Recommended" labelling is gentler than pre-selection.
- **Subscription default. — Judgement.** Decide from the brand's data and preference; whichever is pre-selected, both options must be equally legible and the recurring nature unmistakable. Never grey out the alternative.
- **Selector-level help.** Size guide, "Not sure? We'll change it for you", fit videos, "What's the difference?" links for confusing choices (lab vs natural diamond), shade descriptions ("Screw – a rosewood brown").
- **Add-on price deltas inline** ("Wheels + Feet (+$20)"); show a running total for configurable products.
- **Numbered steps** ("1. Choose your flavour", "2. Choose your frequency") when there are 2+ selectors.
- **Variant-dependent content updates with the selection**: price, specs, stock, images, labels.
- **Honest urgency at variant level** ("Black: 2 left") only from real inventory.
- **Quantity stepper:** inline with the CTA; drop it when pack options do the same job.
- **Every selector comes before the CTA it affects.**

## 7. The add-to-cart CTA and the area around it

- **Prominent, not too early, not too late. — Firm.** It should appear within about the second screen height on mobile, after the readiness gate is met.
- **Sticky add-to-cart** appears only after the shopper scrolls past the inline CTA.
- **Label:** "Add to Cart / Add to Bag" for multi-product stores; "Buy Now" can suit single-hero brands. Keep the price out of the button for single-SKU products (it's in the selected option); show a running total for configurable products.
- **Service USPs directly under the CTA. — Firm.** Delivery time, free shipping (threshold), returns, guarantee, trial. Make them unique, not repeated above. Icons/ticks in one line.
- **Social proof near the CTA. — Firm.** A 3-line customer review (ideally a before→after story, with name and "Verified"), an expert quote, a customer count, press. This is the trust condition at the moment of decision.
- **Unique strengths near the CTA:** store pickup, virtual consultation ("Talk to us"), charity commitments, free installation. Protect these.
- **Offers near the CTA:** free gift with value ("worth $18"), threshold perks.
- **Express checkout. — Default:** keep Shop Pay / wallet buttons but style as an outlined secondary so they don't compete. Removing them is a test, not a fix.
- **Secondary CTAs** (talk to us, quiz) get clearly lower visual weight than the primary.
- **Legal text near the CTA is locked** (auto-renewal disclosures, chat-recording consent). Restyle, never remove.

## 8. Below the CTA: proof, depth, cross-sell

- **"Why you'll love it"** summary bullets before the accordions when benefits weren't shown above.
- **Hero ingredient / feature spotlight** card (one signature ingredient and what it does) — strong for beauty and haircare.
- **USP icon strip** (free-from, cruelty-free, etc.) — icons break the flow and re-engage skimmers.
- **Brand/positioning block** with a headline ("Finally, a dark spot serum made for our complexions"), 2–3 lines, visible (not in an accordion).
- **Cross-sell — Firm.** Logic matched to category: routine in steps (skincare), regime/pairing (haircare), complete the look (décor, fashion), works well with (general). Quick-add buttons, prices visible, optional incentive ("Complete your routine for 10% off"), bundle total ("Add 2 items | ₹710"). Judge on total revenue per visitor.
- **UGC / customer video row — Firm.** Videos have the lowest drop-off; low-risk mid-page. Show different skin tones for beauty, application, texture, real use.
- **Expert endorsements and clinician badges** near the CTA or here.

## 9. Accordions

- **Accordions, not horizontal tabs. — Firm.**
- **Question-style titles. — Firm** (measured higher click-through): "What are the ingredients?", "How to use?", "Who is it for?", "How does it taste?".
- **Category sets:**
  - Ingestibles: benefits, who it's for, ingredients/nutrition, how to take/when to drink, how it tastes.
  - Beauty: product description, benefits, ingredients, how to use/apply, shade guide (colour cosmetics), who is it for.
  - Coffee: what's the roast, origin story, how it tastes, how to brew.
  - High-ticket: story behind the product, details & specs, warranty & returns, care & maintenance.
  - Kits: what's in the pack.
- **Source FAQs from support/sales teams.** Also good for SEO.
- **Place them reasonably close to the CTA**, after the proof/summary blocks.

## 10. Colour and visual hierarchy

- **The CTA colour is scarce. — Firm.** Used only on the primary CTA. Not in announcement bars, icons, review stars, body text, headings or the subscription card fill. List every element using the CTA colour; anything else competes.
- **Brand colour on the edges and selected states; neutral decision zone.** Header and announcement bar can carry the brand colour; the area where shoppers read and choose sits on a calm, readable background.
- **Contrast:** the CTA must stand out *and* meet WCAG AA (4.5:1 for button text). White text on a light colour fails.
- **Type size by hierarchy:** enlarge undersized body text; shrink oversized titles (~24px mobile).
- **Compact white space:** enough to breathe, not so much that it pushes the CTA down.
- **A tinted panel** can group the title block and gallery into one unit.
- **Text colour:** dark neutral for body, not the brand colour.

## 11. Subtraction list

Remove or relocate when present on a mobile PDP:
- second announcement bar; account/login icon; social share icons; "Write a review" link near the top; breadcrumbs on shallow catalogs (judgement: keep on deep catalogs)
- dead selectors (one option); quantity stepper when packs exist
- price inside the CTA (single SKU)
- off-page links in the buying zone (store map, "Shop now") unless they resolve fit
- "Packaging may vary" style disclaimers when non-essential (never legal/safety text)
- frequency dropdowns inside subscription cards when the cadence is fixed
- bare image counters ("1/7") replaced with arrows/thumbnails
- oversized empty space around the hero

## 12. Desktop specifics

- Two columns: gallery grid on the left (hero spanning the width, then a 2-column grid) and a sticky buy box on the right carrying the full mobile decision sequence. The add-to-cart should be visible in, or very near, the first screen.
- Text navigation in one line; no emoji in navigation labels.
- Depth content (ingredients or notes card, question accordions) in two columns below the fold; a 4-up video/UGC row; cross-sell.
- Keep line lengths at 60–75 characters; don't stretch mobile blocks to full width.

## 13. Other page types

### Homepage
Two jobs: build trust and enable relevant product discovery.
- Announcement bar (as above).
- Navigation: up to ~5 shopping categories ordered by revenue; no blog/About Us in the main nav (story goes in a homepage section or secondary menu). Large catalogs: keep the principle, not the number.
- Banners (≤ 3; the first carries most weight): route to the highest-converting funnels by data (best-converting product, top-revenue category, sale page). CTA labels name the destination ("Shop Hydrating Serums", not "Shop Now"). A sale banner states offer, reason and end date.
- Category grid showing the full range (visitors self-select what they want; they learn the range and return for other categories).
- Bestsellers slider with quick-add; product cards: short names, quick add + price, a benefit under 50 characters.
- Optional modules by gap — trust: reviews, press, before/after, backed by science, mini About Us, sustainability; discovery: shop by price, gifts, new arrivals, back-in-stock, shop the look, expanded search, in-cart items highlighted.

### Dedicated landing page vs PDP for ad traffic
- Landing pages (copy, design language and imagery continuing the ad) tend to work for high-consideration, education-heavy, feature-rich products (tech, strollers, furniture).
- For fast decisions (skincare, supplements, fashion) a familiar PDP usually wins.
- Always recommend an A/B test; check ad-to-page message match when the ad creative is available.

### Cart and checkout (light coverage)
Fewer than 10 decision points; guest checkout default; trust signals at checkout; total cost upfront; express wallets; reinforce benefits on the cart (free-shipping progress, samples).
