# Category modules

For each category: the **must-knows** (information a shopper needs before deciding; missing ones are findings), the **top doubts** the hero and first fold must answer, and **signature elements** that recur in strong redesigns. Confidence reflects how many real teardowns support the module.

Pick the closest module. If none fits, reason from the nearest one plus the aesthetic/functional and price-tier axes in `audit-method.md`, and say the module is extrapolated.

## Skincare (treatment, cleansers, serums) — confidence: high
**Must-knows:** skin type and concern it addresses; what it does (benefit) and why (key actives); proof of results (clinical or customer-reported); size (ml) and price; how to use; full ingredients; free-from claims; who it's for.
**Top doubts:** Is it for my skin? Does it actually work? Will it irritate me?
**Signature elements:** concern pills above the title; clinical or customer-result stat on the hero; press/award badge; benefit + active-ingredient bullets in two columns; hero-ingredient spotlight; size cards with savings; "Complete your routine" in numbered steps with checkboxes and a bundle total; before/after in the gallery; UGC across skin tones.
**Watch for:** efficacy claims (anti-ageing, acne, collagen) must already exist in brand materials; keep asterisk qualifiers; cosmetic-vs-drug claim line.

## Sunscreen — confidence: medium
**Must-knows:** SPF/PA rating; filter type (mineral/chemical, photostable); white cast; texture and finish (sticky, greasy, matte); skin type; reapplication; size; origin when it's a trust signal (e.g., K-beauty in India).
**Top doubts:** Will it leave a white cast? Will it feel sticky?
**Signature elements:** objection pills ("No white cast", "Non-sticky"); a lifestyle hero showing the product on skin with a stat ("96% saw no white cast"); benefit + mechanism bullets; bundle-as-variant (sunscreen + moisturiser); review directly under the CTA.

## Colour cosmetics — confidence: medium
**Must-knows:** shade name *and* plain-language description; swatch on real skin across skin tones; finish (matte, satin, gloss); feel and wear (comfort, transfer, longevity); volume; how to apply; ingredients.
**Top doubts:** Which shade is right for me? How will it look on my skin tone?
**Signature elements:** shade selector directly under the gallery; selected shade echoed in the label with a description; sneak peek showing the colour on lips/skin; "Why you'll love it" after the CTA; shade guide accordion; customer videos across skin tones.
**Watch for:** the selected swatch, label and gallery image must agree.

## Haircare — confidence: high
**Must-knows:** hair/scalp type and concern; key active or hero ingredient and what it does; scent (a major purchase driver; put it in the title when signature); size and number of uses; free-from claims (sulfate, paraben, silicone); regimen pairing (conditioner, leave-in); how to use.
**Top doubts:** Is it for my hair type? Will it actually fix my concern? What does it smell like?
**Signature elements:** concern pills; value per use ("300 ml | lasts 60 applications"); customer-result stat on the hero; hero-ingredient card; free-from icon strip; regime cross-sell with bundle price; question-style accordions incl. "Which formula is right for me?".
**Watch for:** scent sections and fragrance notes are often lost in redesigns. Protect them.

## Supplements and functional nutrition — confidence: high
**Must-knows (all five are required):**
1. Form and texture in the title (powder, gummy, syrup, capsule, stick sachet)
2. Flavour in the title, not only in the options
3. Routine: when and how much ("10 ml after breakfast and dinner")
4. Servings / duration next to the price and in each size option ("30-day supply", "24 doses", "$2.28/day")
5. Nutrition label in the gallery and in an accordion ("See nutritional facts" link)
Plus: who it's for (audience, life stage), key benefits, proof, third-party testing/expert formulation.
**Top doubts:** Is it safe? Will I like taking it? Can I fit it into my day? Is it worth it per day? Does it work?
**Signature elements:** concern pills; primary image shows what's inside the box; subscription with reasons (cadence matched to use, e.g., ship around your cycle); size options labelled by purpose ("Best Results", "Travel Friendly"); "Best results in 30 days" linking supply to results; expert/clinician quote near the CTA; standard accordion set (benefits, who it's for, ingredients, how to take, how it tastes).
**Watch for:** the most regulated claims on any page (US FDA structure/function + FTC substantiation; AU TGA; weight-loss and disease claims). Never introduce or escalate health claims. Don't demote strong proof (OBGYN-formulated, third-party tested) below generic claims.

## Food and snacks (incl. better-for-you) — confidence: high
**Must-knows:** what it is and flavour (in the title); pack size/count; headline nutrition facts (protein, sugar, calories, fibre); dietary flags (gluten-free, vegan); full nutrition label link; preparation if any; taste reassurance.
**Top doubts:** Will it taste good? Is it actually healthy (better-for-you)? Which flavour should I pick?
**Signature elements:** "Pack of 8" identity label; "Most re-ordered" badge (consumable-specific proof); headline nutrition bullets + "See nutritional facts"; flavour tiles with images; **variety pack placed next to the default**; numbered selectors; unit price on both frequency options ($4.44/box vs $5.22/box); "How does it taste?" accordion.
**Better-for-you:** land both sides early — indulgent (sensory copy) and healthy (macros). Brand-led pages often land only the first.

## Coffee and tea — confidence: high
**Must-knows:** roast level / blend; tasting notes; format (ground, whole bean, pods) as visual tiles; bag weight next to price; origin; brew method.
**Top doubts:** Will I like the taste? Is it the right grind for my machine?
**Signature elements:** roast/format label above the title; sensory 1–2 line description; attention-catching hero (a pour/ritual shot, or the packshot when packaging is iconic); a roast meter on the image; 6 short feature bullets in two columns; ground vs whole-bean tiles with photos; one-time vs subscribe cards (frame subscription around its real benefit, e.g., free shipping below the threshold); accordions (roast, origin, taste, brew); brand story block condensed but voice-preserving.
**Watch for:** formats advertised on the bag/image must exist in the selector; avoid repeating tasting notes across badge, description and bullets.

## Functional beverages — confidence: medium
**Must-knows:** functional promise (calm, energy, focus) and the ingredient that delivers it; caffeine and sugar; flavour; can/bottle size and count; price per can; when to drink.
**Signature elements:** "0g Caffeine / 0g Sugar" style badges; title with flavour and pack count; per-can price at the top and on every pack card; best-seller claim on the hero (only if true); variety pack as a pack option; "When to drink?" accordion.

## Fragrance (incl. inspired-by / dupe brands) — confidence: medium
**Must-knows:** what it smells like (top / heart / base notes, main accords); concentration (EDT, EDP, Extrait) as the strength proof; longevity in hours and projection; occasion and season; who it's for; size and price per ml; for inspired-by brands, the reference scent in the brand's own wording; returns and cancellation (often non-returnable); dispatch time if made to order; how to apply.
**Top doubts:** Will it smell like the original or like my idea of it? Will it last and will people notice? Is a full bottle a safe blind buy?
**Signature elements:** the inspired-by promise as the first line; a concrete spec bar (hours of longevity, projection, best time); a scent pyramid in text; size tiles with price per ml; a low-risk first try at the selector (smallest size, tester or sample pack); bundle offers; engraving or gift options; customer videos (first-spray reactions, longevity checks); question accordions covering lasting, wearing, applying, ingredients, returns.
**Watch for:** notes listed inconsistently between images and description; vague claims ("super lasting", "beast mode") without the hours; countdowns that don't match the real sale end; showing the original brand's bottle (legal review before giving it prominence); an empty unit-price field (India expects a unit sale price online).

## Kitchenware and home essentials — confidence: medium
**Must-knows:** capacity/dimensions; material and safety (non-toxic, plastic-free, coating-free); heat and care compatibility (microwave, oven, dishwasher, freezer); colour; in-use photos with real food for scale; comparison with alternatives; size relative to siblings in the range.
**Top doubts:** Is it safe for food? Will it fit what I cook? Is it worth more than cheaper alternatives?
**Signature elements:** safety objection pills; spec-led description ("12 cups capacity. 100% plastic free, microwave safe…"); colour swatches under the gallery; pack sets as the AOV lever with per-unit price or savings shown; "Us vs Them" image in the gallery; express checkout as an outlined secondary.

## Home décor and lighting — confidence: medium (aesthetic purchase)
**Must-knows:** dimensions (height, diameter; will it fit my space?); materials (which part is which); for lighting: bulb type, wattage, fitting, cord length, switch; assembly; care; delivery time; damage/returns policy for bulky items; customisation/trade options.
**Top doubts:** Will it look right in my room? Will it fit? What if it arrives damaged?
**Signature elements:** large room-context image first (aesthetic exception to the title-above-gallery rule); clean descriptive title (style + material + colour + name); post-code price shown when a coupon applies; tick-icon highlights; "From the collection" / "Complete the look" cross-sell with quick add; payment-trust and local payment messaging (UPI/COD in India).

## Functional furniture — confidence: medium (functional purchase)
**Must-knows:** adjustment ranges with fit guidance; dimensions; materials/colours; weight capacity; assembly; warranty; trial and returns; the functional benefit backed by authority or mechanism.
**Signature elements:** name includes purpose ("The Original Office Chair"); founder/expert credential in the description; award badge on the hero; workspace lifestyle image; size/fit video at the selector; add-on price deltas inline; running total; honest variant-level stock; "60-day risk-free trial" under the CTA.
**Watch for:** pain/posture claims keep the brand's exact strength ("relieves" never becomes "pain-free").

## Fine jewellery and other high-ticket items — confidence: medium
**Must-knows:** materials and specs (metal, carat, clarity, colour; certification); size and fit with a size guide; scale on the body; lead time (especially made-to-order); warranty; returns/resizing (check made-to-order exceptions); care.
**Top doubts:** Is it worth the price? What if it doesn't fit or something goes wrong? Can I talk to someone?
**Signature elements:** spec line under the title; returns/exchanges in the announcement bar; reassurance at the selector ("Not sure? We'll change it for you"); "What's the difference?" for confusing variants (lab vs natural); secondary "Talk to us" (video consultation) CTA, outlined; on-hand imagery; accordions (story, specs, warranty & returns, care); no aggressive cross-sell or urgency.
**Add (beyond the source teardowns):** support for return visits — wishlist/save, email details, appointment booking, financing.
**Watch for:** specs and price must update with the variant.

## Fashion and apparel — confidence: low (principles only)
Imagery carries most of the persuasion. Likely must-knows: fit and sizing (size guide, model size, fit notes), fabric and feel, colour swatches under the gallery, on-model images across body types, returns/exchanges, how to wear/occasion. Apply the aesthetic-purchase exception (image first) and the variant rules. Say in the audit that this module is extrapolated.

## Electronics and gadgets — confidence: low (principles only)
High consideration, feature-led. Likely must-knows: key specs in plain language, compatibility, what's in the box, warranty, comparison with models in the range, setup. A dedicated landing page may outperform the PDP for ad traffic (recommend testing). Say in the audit that this module is extrapolated.
